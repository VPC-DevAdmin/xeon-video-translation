# Whole-flow profile, 4 October 2026 (commit a53c577)

One fast and one quality job on the 52 s 1080x1920 fixture, warm models, face
track cached. Sampled every second: all eight GPUs (SM, memory, NVENC/NVDEC,
power, clocks), host CPU and memory, per-container CPU; py-spy at 100 Hz on
the fast coordinator, a fast worker, the batch coordinator, a batch worker and
the backend during TTS. Raw data and stacks: `artifacts/bench/profile-2026-10-04/`.
Tooling: `scripts/profiling/{profile_run,analyze_profile,build_report}.py`.

## Binding constraint

The renderer coordinator is a single Python thread that feeds the denoise
worker processes. py-spy self time on it during the fast job:

| Function | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| `prepare_mask_latents` (VAE encode of masked frames, per 16-frame chunk) | 25% |
| `lipsync_pipeline.py:1096` generator (`.to("cpu")` of 5 conditioning tensors per chunk) | 25% |
| `restore_img` (CPU warp and blend of faces back into 1080p frames) | 20% |
| decode, warp, misc | 30% |

Workers spend 56% in `DDIMScheduler.step` (the first CPU call that waits on
the GPU) and 11% in `cuda.synchronize`: GPU-bound, but starved. That is why
the two fast GPUs sit at 63%/48% and why a third would not help yet.

Per 16 s window (fast, 10 steps, 2 GPUs): 37 s waiting on workers, 15 s
conditioning, 13 s decode and warp, 10 s restore, 3 s write.

## Cold start

First request after a renderer restart pays ~75 s: pipeline build 24 s,
worker pool 14 s, insightface ONNX sessions 36 s. The 36 s is pure-Python
protobuf parsing (`protobuf 3.20.3`, implementation `python`) of all five
buffalo_l models (340 MB) although only det_10g and 2d106det are used.

## Changes in payoff order

1. Move the masked-frame VAE encode off the coordinator (batch it per window
   or do it on the worker).
2. Share conditioning tensors as CUDA tensors (IPC) instead of staging through
   host memory; keep fast workers on one NUMA node meanwhile.
3. Paste faces back on the GPU, or at least in a thread pool.
4. insightface `allowed_modules=["detection","landmark_2d_106"]`, protobuf with
   the upb implementation, build the ImageProcessor at startup.
5. Only then add replicas per GPU (VRAM allows two).
6. Bound TTS retakes: three full re-syntheses of a 25 s segment cost 15 s.

## Results of fixes 1-4 (`00a136c`, `8a49ba6`)

Same fixture, fast mode, GPUs 3+5, warm, face track cached.

| Per 16 s window | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| conditioning on coordinator | 15.0 s | 0.6 s |
| waiting on workers (now includes their VAE encode) | 37 s | 47 s |
| decode + warp + audio | 13 s | 13 s |
| restore | 10 s | 10 s |
| window total | 78 s | 75 s |
| first window after restart | 144 s | 74 s |
| mean SM utilization GPU 3 / GPU 5 | 63% / 48% | 79% / 72% (≈440 W each) |
| fast job total | 294 s | 282 s |

What this means: the coordinator stopped being the feeder bottleneck. The
VAE work did not disappear, it moved onto the GPUs that were waiting, so
wall time barely changed but utilization rose 20 points and the stage now
scales with worker count. Adding a third fast GPU (or a second worker per
GPU, VRAM allows it) is the next step, where before it would have been wasted.

Restore batching (fix 3) did not help: the per-frame kornia warp, erosion,
CPU cv2.erode and Gaussian blur chain is ~24 ms a frame regardless of how
frames travel. A real fix batches those kernels across frames with a shared
erosion radius or moves the paste into the worker alongside the VAE decode.

Cold start (fix 4): detector sessions 36 s -> 1.3 s by trimming the
buffalo_l pack to the two models used; pipeline, pool and sessions are now
built at service start (31.7 s, `LATENTSYNC_WARMUP=1`) so the first request
pays nothing extra.
